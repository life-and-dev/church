# Instant Heaven

Many Christians believe that the *immortal soul* of a Christian goes *immediately and conciously to heaven to live with God*. However, this is also not biblical. While it is unclear where this idea originates, the striking parallel with Greek philosophy and Roman mythology may have influenced this Christian belief.

## Immortal Soul

Greek philosophy offered accounts of the soul that influenced modern Christianity. [Plato's Phaedo](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0170%3Atext%3DPhaedo%3Apage%3D70) presents arguments for ***the soul's survival after death***. In [Phaedrus 245c](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0174%3Atext%3DPhaedrus%3Asection%3D245c), Plato states: ***“Every soul is immortal.”*** Tertullian, one of the greatest influencers on Christianity, appeals to Plato in [On the Resurrection of the Flesh 3](https://www.tertullian.org/anf/anf03/anf03-41.htm): *“I may use, therefore, the opinion of a Plato, when he declares, ‘**[Every soul is immortal](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/immortal)**.’”* He uses Plato's opinion to corroborate a shared claim. However, Tertullian's work also defends [bodily resurrection](death/resurrection.md).

Early Christian writers did not all agree on the soul's immortality. In [Dialogue with Trypho 5–6](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/01281.htm), an old man argues that **souls are not immortal by nature but live by God's will**, and Justin accepts his argument. The dialogue also says some souls remain for [judgment](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/judgement.md), so dependence on God does not rule out survival after death.

- **Innate immortality** means possessing immortality by nature.
- **Conditional immortality** makes lasting life dependent on God.

Later, [Augustine](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120113.htm) describes ***the soul as immortal in the sense that it continues to live and feel***, and says that **the souls of the righteous rest after death**. He also defends [bodily resurrection](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120122.htm).

Christian Tornau's [article abstract in *Phronesis* 62.3 (2017)](https://brill.com/view/journals/phro/62/3/article-p319_5.xml) argues that Augustine constructed his proof of the ***soul's immortality*** in *Soliloquies* 2.22–24 from several passages in Porphyry's works on Aristotle's *Categories*.

The Bible does not require the conclusion that every person possesses a soul that cannot die. Genesis 2:7 describes the man becoming a living soul, while Jesus warns that **God can destroy both soul and body** (Matthew 10:28). The hope of lasting life rests on God's gift, not [an inherently indestructible human soul](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/immortal.md) (Romans 6:23).

## Heaven

In Plato's [Republic, Book 10](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~bmaclenn/Classes/US310/rep.X.614B-621D.html), the Myth of Er describes ***souls travelling after death to a place where judges send them upward or downward***. In contrast, Daniel 12:2 describes the dead as **sleeping in the dust until they awaken**, and John 5:28-29 describes a future time when those in the tombs will come out. Plato's account also teaches ***reincarnation***, not [resurrection](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/resurrection.md) through Christ.

In [Timaeus 41-42](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/timaeus.html), Plato assigns souls to stars. Those who live well return to their star, while others undergo successive lives. Likewise, Cicero's [Dream of Scipio](https://www.attalus.org/cicero/republic6.html), promises ***a place in heaven and eternal life to those who serve their country***. These accounts resemble the idea of a heavenly reward after death, but their conditions and details differ from Christian teaching.

Greek mythology also describes ***exceptional humans living among the gods***. In [Pindar's Olympian 2](https://web.as.miami.edu/personal/corax/pindar.html), Semele lives among the Olympians after her death. This resembles the idea of a person entering a divine realm, but it is not a general promise for ordinary people. Many modern Christians believe as well that they will live among *angelical beings in Heaven*.

Although Scripture describes heaven as God's dwelling (Psalm 11:4) and some Christians understand Paul's desire to “depart and be with Christ” (Philippians 1:23) and his words about being “away from the body and at home with the Lord” (2 Corinthians 5:8) as teaching conscious life in heaven immediately after death. Another reading is that Paul describes the believer's eventual hope without explaining an experience between death and resurrection. Neither passage explicitly describes an immortal soul entering heaven at death. [The resurrection](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/resurrection.md) remains central to Paul's account of how the dead receive life.

Paul also explicitly says believers will meet the returning Lord in the clouds (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17). The passage promises that they will be with him, but does not describe their permanent destination.

The popular picture of heaven also includes clouds, gates and gold. Homer describes [gates of heaven](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0134%3Abook%3D8%3Acard%3D381) used by the gods. Revelation 21:2,21 describes the New Jerusalem coming down out of heaven, with gates of pearl and a street of gold, but it does not describe souls entering these gates immediately after death.

## Conclusion

Scripture does not teach that an immortal soul enters heaven at death. The Christian hope is [resurrection](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/life/resurrection). Rather than expecting to leave the earth and live forever in heaven. Believers can look forward to being raised to life in God's renewed creation (the new earth) where God will dwell with His people (Revelation 21:1-3).
