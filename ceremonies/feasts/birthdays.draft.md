# The Origins and Biblical Perspective of Birthdays

While birthdays are widely celebrated today with cake, candles, and parties, the
historical and biblical records of these events tell a different story. Many
people participate in these annual traditions without knowing where they began
or how they were viewed by early believers. This exploration delves into the
origins of common birthday customs and examines the scriptural accounts
associated with such celebrations.

## Biblical Accounts of Birthdays

The Bible contains several references to birthday celebrations, though they are
often associated with individuals who did not follow the God of Israel.

* The apostle Paul instructed believers to stand firm and hold to the
  traditions they were taught by the apostles (2 Thessalonians 2:15, 3:6 (ESV)).
* Pharaoh celebrated his birthday, which many historians believe was actually a
  celebration of his "birth" as a god. This event resulted in the execution of
  his chief baker (Genesis 40:20-22 (ESV)).
* Herod Antipas held a banquet for his birthday, during which the daughter of
  Herodias danced and requested the head of John the Baptist. This led to the
  execution of the prophet (Matthew 14:6-11; Mark 6:21-28 (ESV)).
* Job's children held feasts on their specific days, which Job feared might lead
  them to sin. Some scholars interpret these "days" as birthdays (Job 1:4-5
  (ESV)).

## Historical Origins of Birthday Customs

> According to Funk & Wagnalls Standard Dictionary of Folklore, Mythology, and
> Legend, these celebrations originated from the belief that on a person’s
> birthday, “evil spirits and influences have the opportunity to attack the
> celebrants” and that “the presence of friends and the expression of good
> wishes help to protect the celebrant.” The book The Lore of Birthdays says
> that in ancient times, birthday records were “essential for the casting of a
> horoscope” based on “the mystic science of astrology.” This book adds that
> “birthday candles, in folk belief, are endowed with special magic for
> granting wishes.” — [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://www.jw.org/en/jehovahs-witnesses/faq/birthdays/)

The Bible warns against practices associated with omens and sorcery
(Deuteronomy 18:14; Galatians 5:19-21 (ESV)).

## Views of the Early Church

> The early Christians did not celebrate birthdays. The World Book Encyclopedia
> says that “they considered the celebration of anyone’s birth to be a pagan
> custom.” — [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://www.jw.org/en/jehovahs-witnesses/faq/birthdays/)

## Generosity and Gift Giving

Scripture encourages spontaneous generosity and the giving of good gifts without
being tied to specific calendar dates.

> Therefore, if you, although being wicked, know how to give good gifts to your
> children, how much more so will your Father who is in the heavens give good
> things to those asking him! — Matthew 7:11 (ESV)

## Experiences of Non-Celebrants

Some children who do not celebrate birthdays describe the joy of receiving
unexpected gifts throughout the year.

> “It’s more fun getting a gift when you’re least expecting it.” — Tammy, age 12
>
> “Even though I don’t get presents on my birthday, my parents still buy me
> gifts on other occasions. I like it that way because I get surprised.” —
> Gregory, age 11
>
> “Do you think that ten minutes, a few cupcakes, and a song make a party? You
> should come to my house and see what a real party is like!” — Eric, age 6

## Conclusion

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->
The study of birthdays reveals a complex history rooted in ancient pagan traditions and offerings to deities. While the Bible does not explicitly command or forbid birthday celebrations, the [biblical accounts of birthdays](#biblical-accounts-of-birthdays) are consistently associated with negative outcomes and individuals outside the faith. [Historical origins](#historical-origins-of-birthday-customs) suggest that many modern customs, such as candles and cakes, have their roots in ancient superstitions. [Early church views](#views-of-the-early-church) show a general rejection of these customs in favor of [scriptural generosity](#generosity-and-gift-giving). Ultimately, the [experiences of non-celebrants](#experiences-of-non-celebrants) demonstrate that meaningful celebration and gift-giving can flourish independently of these traditional dates.
