# Feast of the Passover (Pesach)

The Feast of the Passover, or Pesach, is one of the most significant
observances in the biblical calendar. It marks the beginning of the festival
season and serves as a powerful reminder of liberation and divine protection.
This article explores the historical roots, specific requirements, and the
distinct nature of this solemn night as established in the scriptures.

## Historical Institution

The Passover was established by the LORD during the Exodus from Egypt. It was a
night of solemn observance to commemorate the LORD bringing the Israelites out
of the land of Egypt.

> It is a night of solemn observance to the LORD for bringing them out of the land of Egypt. — Exodus 12:42 (ESV)

This feast was intended to be a permanent statute for all generations
(Exodus 12:43 (ESV)). It serves as a memorial of the time when the LORD passed
over the houses of the Israelites while striking the firstborn of the Egyptians
(Exodus 12:27 (ESV)).

## Biblical Timing and Requirements

The Passover occurs on the fourteenth day of the first month at twilight
(Leviticus 23:5 (ESV)). While it is often associated with the seven-day Feast
of Unleavened Bread, the fourteenth day itself is specifically identified as
the LORD's Passover (Numbers 28:16 (ESV)). In the New Testament, Jesus is
referred to as the Passover lamb who has been sacrificed
(1 Corinthians 5:7 (ESV)).

Specific regulations were given for its observance:
- The meal must be eaten in a single house and not taken outside
  (Exodus 12:46 (ESV)).
- No bones of the sacrificial lamb are to be broken (Exodus 12:46 (ESV)). This
  was also fulfilled in the crucifixion of Jesus (John 19:36 (ESV)).
- Participation is restricted to those who are circumcised, including both
  native Israelites and strangers who wish to join the community
  (Exodus 12:43-45,48-49 (ESV)).
- The same laws regarding the Passover apply to both the native-born and the
  stranger who sojourns among the people (Exodus 12:49 (ESV)).

## Distinction from Unleavened Bread

It is important to distinguish between the Passover and the Feast of Unleavened
Bread. The Passover is the sacrifice on the fourteenth day. The Feast of
Unleavened Bread begins on the fifteenth day and lasts for seven days
(Leviticus 23:5-6 (ESV)).

Confusion sometimes arises regarding which days are
[holy convocations](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+23&version=ESV).
A holy convocation is a sacred assembly where no ordinary work is permitted.
The first day of the Feast of Unleavened Bread (the fifteenth) and the seventh
day (the twenty-first) are designated as holy convocations
(Exodus 12:16 (ESV); Numbers 28:17-18 (ESV)). The fourteenth day, the Passover,
is the day of preparation and the sacrifice, but it is not listed as a holy
convocation for rest in the same way as the first and last days of Unleavened
Bread.

## Conclusion

The Feast of the Passover remains a foundational pillar of biblical tradition,
emphasizing obedience, remembrance, and the distinction between the sacred and
the profane. Through its [historical institution](#historical-institution) and
[specific requirements](#biblical-timing-and-requirements), it continues to
point to the LORD's intervention in human history. The
[distinction from the Feast of Unleavened Bread](#distinction-from-unleavened-bread)
ensures that each appointed time is honored according to its unique purpose as
a [statute forever](https://www.esv.org/Exodus+12/).
